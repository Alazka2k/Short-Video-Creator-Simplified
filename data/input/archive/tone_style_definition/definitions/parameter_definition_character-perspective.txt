## Explaination and Definition
### Documentary Videos
- Expert:
Provides authoritative insights and analysis (e.g., scientists, historians, industry leaders).
- Eyewitness:
Offers first-hand accounts of events (e.g., participants in historical events, local residents).
- Affected Individual:
Shares personal experiences related to the topic (e.g., patients in medical documentaries, environmental impact victims).

### Educational/Instructional Videos
- Instructor:
Guides learners through concepts and skills (e.g., teachers, trainers, coaches).
- Student/Learner:
Demonstrates the learning process, asks questions (e.g., on-screen learner, relatable character).
- Practitioner:
Shows real-world application of knowledge (e.g., professionals in their field, experienced craftspeople).

### Promotional Videos
- Satisfied Customer:
Provides testimonials and success stories (e.g., product users, service beneficiaries).
- Brand Ambassador:
Represents and embodies the brand's values (e.g., celebrities, influencers, company representatives).
- Problem Solver:
Demonstrates how the product/service addresses needs (e.g., fictional characters facing common issues).

### Narrative Films
- Protagonist:
Drives the main story and undergoes character development (e.g., hero, main character).
- Antagonist:
Provides conflict and challenges for the protagonist (e.g., villain, opposing force).
- Supporting Character:
Adds depth and context to the story (e.g., sidekick, mentor, love interest).

### Corporate/Business Videos
- CEO/Executive:
Provides vision and leadership perspective (e.g., company founders, top management).
- Employee:
Offers insights into company culture and operations (e.g., team members, department heads).
- Client/Partner:
Shares experiences of working with the company (e.g., business partners, long-term clients).

### Vlogs/Personal Videos
- Content Creator:
Shares personal experiences and opinions (e.g., vlogger, influencer).
- Guest:
Provides additional perspectives or expertise (e.g., friends, collaborators, interviewees).
- Audience Stand-in:
Represents the viewer's perspective (e.g., character asking questions viewers might have).
