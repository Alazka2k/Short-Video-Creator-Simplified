## Explaination and Definition
### Narrative Films
- Three-Act Structure: 
Slow build-up in Act 1, increasing tension in Act 2, and rapid climax in Act 3.
- Varying Scene Lengths: 
Quick cuts for energy, longer takes for depth, mixing it up to prevent monotony1.

#### Documentaries
- Measured Pacing: 
Slower, more consistent rhythm to allow for information absorption.
- Alternating Tempos: 
Slower rhythms for reflection, quicker tempos for high-energy segments.

### Action/Thriller
Fast-Paced Rhythm: Quick cuts, dynamic camera movements, and rapid scene transitions to heighten tension.
Contrast: Alternating between fast and slow pacing to create unpredictability and keep viewers engaged.

### Comedy
- Snappy Pacing: 
Rapid-fire dialogues and precise editing to land jokes effectively.
- Well-Timed Pauses: 
Strategic use of pauses to enhance comedic timing2.

### Educational/Training Videos
Balanced Pacing: 
- Alternating between fast-paced, high-energy segments and slower, more informative sections1.
Deliberate Pacing: 
- Slower pace for complex concepts, allowing viewers to absorb information.

### Promotional Videos
Energetic Pacing: Faster and more dynamic to generate enthusiasm and encourage action.
Music-Driven Rhythm: Using upbeat music combined with quick-paced editing to create momentum
